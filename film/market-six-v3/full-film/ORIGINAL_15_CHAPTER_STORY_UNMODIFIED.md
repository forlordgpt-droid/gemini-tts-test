# 100 DAYS INSIDE AN ABANDONED SUPERMARKET
**15-Minute Survival Mystery Movie**

**Genre:** Disaster, Survival, Suspense, Mystery  
**Location:** Bellport, a fictional American town  
**Storytelling:** Approximately 90% narration, 10% dialogue  
**Main characters:** Mason, Clara, Jonah

---

## CHAPTER 1 — 00:00–01:00
### DAY 100: THE DOOR

**VISUAL:** BLACK SCREEN. Heavy metal banging. A supermarket loading shutter shakes violently. Floodwater rises outside. A trembling hand reaches towards a red emergency latch.

**NARRATOR:**

Day one hundred.

You are standing inside a supermarket that should have saved your life.

Now, nineteen people are pounding on the loading-bay shutter.

Water is rising behind them.

Inside, eleven survivors are waiting for you to decide.

Pull the red latch, and the shutter opens.

So does the flood.

Keep it locked, and the people outside may not last another ten minutes.

Clara grips your arm.

Her brother Jonah is counting the last sealed water bottles.

On the radio, a broken voice repeats one warning:

**RADIO:** "Do not open the lower door."

**NARRATOR:**

You look at the latch. You look at the clock.

And you remember how this started—with a normal shopping trip, a little rain, and one terrible decision.

One hundred days earlier, you walked into this store for bread.

You never imagined you'd become the person deciding who gets to live inside it.

**SFX:** A final thunderous BANG.

**CUT TO:** Bright daylight, one hundred days earlier.

---

## CHAPTER 2 — 01:00–02:00
### DAY 1: THE WATER ARRIVES

**VISUAL:** A sunny supermarket parking lot. An emergency alert flashes across a phone. Dark rain clouds approach. Cars become trapped in rising water.

**NARRATOR:**

It's 4:17 in the afternoon.

Your name is Mason, and you are buying bread before driving home.

Then every phone in the store begins screaming.

Emergency alert.

River barrier failure.

Move to higher ground.

In the parking lot, water rushes between the cars.

A delivery truck tips onto its side.

The bridge that leads out of town disappears beneath brown water.

You run back inside as the automatic doors jam halfway open.

Behind you, a woman shouts for her child.

You turn to help, but a security worker pulls them through another exit.

Seconds later, the glass doors explode inward.

You climb the stairs to the stockroom mezzanine.

When the noise finally fades, you look down at an empty store.

No staff. No customers.

Just hundreds of aisles and the sound of water striking the walls.

You have food everywhere.

But the first thing you need isn't food.

**SFX:** The supermarket lights flicker three times.

Then everything goes black.

---

## CHAPTER 3 — 02:00–03:00
### DAY 2: THE FREEZERS DIE

**VISUAL:** Ice cream starts melting. Water drips beneath freezer doors. A digital thermometer climbs. Mason searches dark aisles with a flashlight.

**NARRATOR:**

The grid is down.

The store's backup lights last only a few hours.

Before dawn, the freezers begin warming.

You stare at meat, milk and frozen food worth thousands of dollars, and make your first survival decision.

Stop treating the supermarket like a shop.

Treat it like a shelter.

You leave the fridge and freezer doors closed, but you know they cannot protect the food forever.

Once perishable food has been kept too warm for too long, cooking it later does not automatically make it safe.

You mark the food you can trust.

Unopened cans.

Dry beans.

Rice.

Sealed crackers.

You gather batteries, first-aid supplies, blankets and a hand-crank radio.

Then you smell smoke near the loading bay.

Somebody has left a portable generator running in a small room.

You shut it down and move away.

Its invisible exhaust could kill you before hunger ever does.

**VISUAL:** A shopping cart begins rolling slowly down an empty aisle.

**SFX:** Its wheels squeak.

But nobody is pushing it.

---

## CHAPTER 4 — 03:00–04:00
### DAY 5: THE REAL CURRENCY

**VISUAL:** A dripping tap stops running. Rows of sealed water bottles. A whiteboard showing water calculations. Rain falls onto rooftop containers.

**NARRATOR:**

By day five, the taps are dry.

Now the supermarket's biggest problem is clear.

Aisles of food do not matter if you have nothing safe to drink.

You count roughly six hundred liters of sealed drinking water.

At a basic planning allowance of four liters per person per day for drinking and minimal hygiene, that's about one hundred and fifty days for one person.

Sounds comfortable.

But the numbers change the moment someone else arrives.

So you set up clean containers for rainwater, keeping them separate from drinking water until it can be properly treated.

You refuse to use the muddy floodwater outside.

You label every bottle, every meal, every battery.

And each morning, you write the date on the manager's whiteboard.

It gives the days a shape.

It gives you something to control.

But by the eleventh morning, the numbers on that board no longer match what is on the shelves.

**VISUAL:** The camera pushes towards an inventory sheet.

Then towards an empty space between canned beans.

**ON-SCREEN TEXT:** THREE CANS MISSING.

---

## CHAPTER 5 — 04:00–05:00
### DAY 11: SOMEBODY IS EATING

**VISUAL:** Two photographs of the same shelf. Food appears in one and is missing in the other. A flashlight illuminates a fresh shoeprint.

**NARRATOR:**

Three cans of beans are gone.

You know because you counted them twice.

At first, you blame yourself.

You were tired. You were frightened.

Maybe you moved them and forgot.

Then two packets of biscuits disappear.

And a bottle of water.

None of the external doors has been opened.

The lower loading bay is still blocked by flood debris.

Somebody is inside.

You walk slowly down aisle six, passing bright cereal boxes that look ridiculous in the dark.

Your flashlight catches a shoeprint beside the shelves.

Small.

Recent.

Not yours.

**MASON:** "I don't want to hurt you."

**NARRATOR:**

No reply.

The ceiling makes a soft metallic click.

You look up just in time to see a service hatch settling back into place.

That night, you barricade your sleeping space.

But you can't sleep.

A supermarket full of food suddenly feels smaller than a prison.

**SFX:** Tap. Tap. Tap.

Three slow knocks come from above the ceiling.

---

## CHAPTER 6 — 05:00–06:00
### DAY 24: THE CAMERA DOESN'T LIE

**VISUAL:** A security monitor flickers to life. Solar battery indicator. A thin line of flour across the aisle. Infrared footage showing a moving figure.

**NARRATOR:**

You remember the small solar-charged battery used by the store's security desk.

It still has enough power for one camera and a screen, but not all night.

You angle the camera toward aisle six.

On the floor, you spread a narrow line of flour.

At 2:14 a.m., a figure steps into view.

Not a monster.

Not a ghost.

A person in a red jacket carrying two cans.

The figure hesitates, sees the camera, and runs.

You follow the disturbed flour to a locked maintenance stairwell behind the shelves.

The door is bolted from your side.

You pull it open, expecting an attacker.

Instead, you hear someone coughing in the dark.

**UNKNOWN VOICE:** "Please don't send us outside."

**NARRATOR:**

Us.

That single word is worse than the missing food.

You thought you were protecting one shelter from one thief.

You have no idea how many people are hiding behind that door.

**VISUAL:** A flashlight illuminates a frightened face.

Then a second pair of eyes appears below the stairs.

---

## CHAPTER 7 — 06:00–07:00
### DAY 24: TWO MORE MOUTHS

**VISUAL:** A woman and a young boy emerge from the maintenance stairwell. The boy wears a dusty red jacket. Three plates are set on a table.

**NARRATOR:**

Her name is Clara.

She cleaned this store at night, and she knew about the staff stairwell nobody else used.

The boy beside her is Jonah, her younger brother.

They came here when the flood warning began.

They stayed hidden because they heard you moving through the dark and thought you might hurt them.

You want to be angry.

Instead, you notice the cracked skin on Jonah's lips.

You offer him clean water, slowly, and sit them beneath the emergency lantern.

Clara apologizes for taking food.

Then you do the calculation that every survivor hates.

Six hundred liters sounded like a fortune when you were alone.

For three people, the same amount would cover only fifty days at that planning rate—if you had not already used some.

Food is still abundant.

Safe water is not.

You divide the shelf space into shared supplies and a small emergency reserve.

For the first time in weeks, someone says goodnight.

**VISUAL:** As Clara falls asleep, Jonah quietly unfolds a handwritten paper.

Eight names are written on it.

---

## CHAPTER 8 — 07:00–08:00
### DAY 32: THE LIST

**VISUAL:** Close-up of eight handwritten names. Clara quickly takes the paper. Through a rain-covered window, an abandoned bus depot stands across the flooded road.

**NARRATOR:**

The names are not imaginary friends.

They're people.

Eight survivors, including an older woman who can barely walk, are stranded on the upper floor of the bus depot beyond the flooded road.

Clara has been leaving supplies by an old covered passage whenever the water level allows it.

She never told you.

**CLARA:** "You would have stopped me."

**NARRATOR:**

She's right.

Or at least you think you would have.

You stand at the upstairs window and see a white cloth moving in the depot window.

A signal.

Someone waiting for help.

Here is the problem with rationing.

Every resource you save for tomorrow can be the thing someone else needs today.

But giving away supplies without counting them can also destroy a shelter.

You shut the ledger.

You have a choice.

Protect the three of you or find a way to help eight strangers without endangering everyone.

Then the white cloth stops moving.

**CLARA:** "That isn't their normal signal."

**VISUAL:** The distant window goes dark.

---

## CHAPTER 9 — 08:00–09:00
### DAY 41: THE SILENT DEPOT

**VISUAL:** Binocular view of the bus depot. An SOS light flashes from the supermarket roof. A shadow collapses against the distant window.

**NARRATOR:**

For nine days, you watch the depot.

Some mornings, the white cloth appears.

Other mornings, nothing.

You try the radio, the rooftop flashlight, every safe signal you can make.

Then, through binoculars, you see a man collapse near the glass.

Clara wants to run straight across the flooded road.

You stop her.

Floodwater can hide open drains, sharp debris and a current stronger than it looks.

You study the building plans instead.

Behind the supermarket is an elevated service walkway joining two buildings, away from the flooded street.

From your roof, you can see that part of it remains in place, though you cannot assume it's safe.

You signal the people inside and ask them to wait while you check access.

A reply finally flashes back.

Three short bursts.

Followed by a word written on cardboard.

**VISUAL:** The camera zooms towards the cardboard.

**ON-SCREEN TEXT:** WATER.

**NARRATOR:**

You have found eight survivors.

And you may have found eight people who cannot wait much longer.

**HOOK:** The depot window goes dark again.

---

## CHAPTER 10 — 09:00–10:00
### DAY 50: THE PRICE OF OPENING UP

**VISUAL:** People are guided into the supermarket along the inspected elevated route. Eleven cups appear on a table. A new inventory sheet replaces the old one.

**NARRATOR:**

By day fifty, the survivors from the depot are inside the supermarket.

You only bring them over when the elevated route is checked and conditions are calm.

They are weak, hungry and frightened.

Eleven people now share your shelter.

This is when you discover that survival is not just about supplies.

It's about rules.

One person cooks in a safe, ventilated area.

Another manages waste and sanitation.

Clara takes charge of first aid.

You organize water and food.

Every meal is counted.

Every bottle is recorded.

You learn who can repair things, who can read maps, and who can keep children calm.

Dividing the work saves energy and arguments.

But that evening, you catch a survivor slipping two sealed bottles beneath a mattress.

Your first instinct is to call it theft.

Then you learn they are saving the bottles for a sick relative.

Fear is making people keep secrets again.

**VISUAL:** Clara studies the water ledger.

**CLARA:** "We need to count again."

**CUT TO BLACK.**

---

## CHAPTER 11 — 10:00–11:00
### DAY 61: THE TWELVE-DAY CLOCK

**VISUAL:** Nearly empty water pallets. Eleven survivors study a whiteboard. Clean rain-collection material is arranged on the roof. Dark clouds gather.

**NARRATOR:**

Twelve days.

That is how long the sealed water will last eleven people at the current ration.

The number is not a threat.

It is a fact, and facts are easier to handle when everyone sees them.

You gather the group and tell them the truth.

Then you show them the rain collection system.

It needs more capacity, cleaner surfaces and proper treatment.

Rainwater can carry contamination from roofs, storage containers and the air.

Looking clear does not mean it is safe to drink.

So you use clean catchment materials, keep dirty first runoff out, and treat collected water before putting it into drinking containers.

Everyone helps.

When the next storm breaks, water pounds onto the roof and flows toward the barrels.

Slowly, the gauges begin to rise.

For the first time, survival stops feeling like a countdown.

And then the hand-crank radio wakes up with a message you haven't heard before.

**RADIO:** "Bellport residents, prepare for a second flood crest."

**VISUAL:** Everyone stops moving.

---

## CHAPTER 12 — 11:00–12:00
### DAY 72: THE SECOND WARNING

**VISUAL:** A river map pinned to the wall. Dark clouds overhead. Water strikes the loading dock. Supermarket carts carry emergency supplies upstairs.

**NARRATOR:**

A second flood crest.

You rewind the message in your mind.

The announcer names the river upstream, the same river that swallowed the bridge on day one.

This may be an old recording, but the rain gauge outside is climbing, and the current has changed direction around the loading bay.

You do not wait for certainty.

You move clean water, dry food, blankets and medicines away from the ground floor.

You put the sleeping area above the expected waterline.

Jonah helps by pushing a cart stacked with sealed biscuits.

Clara marks every safe exit on a store plan.

No one tries to run a fuel generator inside.

A power shortage is dangerous, but carbon monoxide is worse.

At sunset, the building shakes under a deep impact.

A floating shipping container has struck the loading dock.

Your careful shelter has survived seventy-two days.

It might not survive seventy-three.

**SFX:** CRACK!

**VISUAL:** A long crack appears across the loading dock wall.

---

## CHAPTER 13 — 12:00–13:00
### DAY 84: THE WALL

**VISUAL:** Water leaks through a concrete crack. Food boxes move towards the upper mezzanine. A handmade distress banner unfolds across the roof.

**NARRATOR:**

The lower wall begins to leak.

Not a dramatic wave.

Just a thin line of water spreading beneath the pallets.

You know what that means.

The supermarket is no longer a place you can trust without checking.

The group moves essentials upstairs.

They build a small, organized shelter on the upper level, away from electrical hazards and unstable shelving.

You paint HELP — 11 PEOPLE onto a large sheet and secure it where aircraft can see it.

Days pass.

The wall holds, but the lower floor remains unsafe.

Then Jonah sees movement on the distant ridge.

A line of people.

Too many to count.

Some are carrying bags.

One is pushing a wheelchair.

The crowd stops at the flooded access road and turns toward the supermarket.

Clara looks at your supply board.

You both understand the same thing.

Your store is the last visible dry building on their route.

**VISUAL:** One stranger raises a red emergency flag.

---

## CHAPTER 14 — 13:00–14:00
### DAY 96: THE WRONG ENTRANCE

**VISUAL:** An old loading-bay shutter. Red warning markings. A higher staff entrance. Mason paints a large sign while rain strikes the supermarket roof.

**NARRATOR:**

You use the next twelve days to prepare.

The lower loading bay is damaged.

Opening it during high water could flood the stockrooms and trap everybody inside.

The upper staff entrance connects to a raised walkway on the dry side of the building.

You paint huge arrows on a sheet.

UPPER ENTRANCE.

You hang it where the crowd can see.

You mark the broken dock with a red cross.

You have room upstairs, but supplies will be tight, and the storm is coming back.

On day ninety-six, the distant group disappears behind the trees.

You think they have found another way.

On day ninety-nine, you hear a signal horn near the ridge.

At dawn on day one hundred, nineteen people appear at the bottom of the loading dock.

They are looking at the wrong door.

You race to the emergency intercom, but its battery is dead.

Then the pounding begins.

**SFX:** BANG! BANG! BANG!

**VISUAL:** Water rises behind the people.

The lower shutter begins bending inward.

---

## CHAPTER 15 — 14:00–15:00
### DAY 100: THE LAST SAFE DOOR

**VISUAL:** Return to the opening scene. The red emergency latch. Rising floodwater. Nineteen frightened survivors. Mason's hand slowly moves away from the latch.

**NARRATOR:**

Nineteen people outside.

Eleven inside.

Your hand is on the emergency latch.

You take it away.

Open that door and rising water will swallow the stockroom.

You run upstairs.

Clara grabs a bright orange sheet.

Jonah spots the higher walkway.

Together you signal the strangers toward the upper entrance, away from the flood.

One by one, they climb.

You help the last woman inside just as water slams against the loading bay below.

Nobody cheers.

They are too exhausted.

You count again.

Thirty people.

The supermarket kept one man alive for one hundred days.

Now it must shelter thirty.

A newcomer puts a working radio on the table.

A clear voice breaks through.

**RADIO:** "Bellport relief convoy to Market Six. Four hundred more survivors are approaching your ridge. How many can you feed?"

**NARRATOR:**

You look at the remaining food.

Then at the door.

For the first time in one hundred days, you have no number to write on the board.

**VISUAL:** Slow camera push towards the almost-empty food ledger.

**SFX:** One slow radio beep.

CUT TO BLACK.
